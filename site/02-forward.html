The Forward Pass

A neural network's forward pass is not magic and it is not even complicated. It is a fixed number of multiplies, a fixed number of adds, and one comparison against zero. Here is every single one of them, in order, for a network small enough to hold in your head.

Every multiply, one at a time

The model is . Step through it below, or press Play. Each hidden unit is a dot product of one row of W1 with the input vector, plus a bias. The diagram lights the exact wire being used; the ledger shows the exact arithmetic; the tensors on the right fill in as the values become real.

The same thing as one matrix multiply

Three separate dot products is how you explain it. It is not how you compute it. All three rows read the same input vector, and none of them depends on the others, so all three can run at once. Stack them and the whole layer is a single matrix–vector product:
