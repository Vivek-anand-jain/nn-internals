What actually happens inside a neural network

A thirteen-parameter network, traced number by number through the forward pass, the backward pass, and the optimizer step — then scaled to 70 billion parameters until the reason for ZeRO, FSDP, tensor parallelism and pipeline parallelism becomes arithmetic rather than jargon.

The running example

One example carries the whole site. Small enough that every single number fits on screen; big enough to contain a real matrix multiply, a real nonlinearity, and a real chain rule. We predict a house price from two features.

Four kinds of tensor. That's the whole story.

Everything in GPU memory during training is one of four things. They have different sizes, different lifetimes, and different reasons to exist. Once you can name which is which, every distributed-training technique becomes obvious — each one is just a different answer to "which of these four do we split up?"

Part I — The mechanics

What a parameter is, what happens to it in a training step, and what that costs in memory. Everything here is traced on the 13-parameter model above, which is small enough that no step has to be summarised.

Part II — A real transformer

The same four tensor classes, now on an architecture you would actually train: a two-layer block with multi-head causal attention, pre-LayerNorm and a GELU MLP, 288 parameters. Forward and backward come first, because you cannot reason about what a transformer costs until you have seen what it computes. Part II ends with a model that does not fit on any single GPU.

Part III — Many GPUs

The answers to Part II's memory wall. The mechanics are worked on a third toy — a 193-parameter network on four simulated GPUs — where every strategy is proven to compute exactly what one GPU would. Then page 22 applies all of it back to the real transformer, matrix by matrix.

Reading paths

Almost nobody reads 01 → 34 in order. Five routes that stand on their own, each one the shortest honest way to a particular question.

Part IV — Inference

A different machine. Training's organising idea was four tensor classes; inference's is arithmetic intensity — FLOPs per byte moved. Decode reads every weight in the model to produce one token, and leaves over 99% of the GPU's arithmetic idle. Every technique here attacks that.

Part V — Mixture of Experts

Parameters and FLOPs stop being the same question. Also the only place all-to-all — taught in Part III and unused since — finally earns its keep.

Every strategy, verified

It is easy to describe distributed training and be subtly wrong. So code/parallel_toy.py simulates four GPUs running one real training step under each strategy and checks the result against the single-GPU answer. Nothing on those pages is asserted where it could be demonstrated.

How this is kept honest

Every number on every page — in the prose, the animations, the tables and the slides — is read from assets/data/trace.js, which is generated by code/ground_truth.py. No value is typed by hand twice, so the visualisations cannot drift away from the mathematics.

The ground-truth script derives all thirteen gradients by hand, with no autograd, then checks them against central finite differences. That check is reported below and runs every time the data is regenerated.

Regenerate everything with python3 code/ground_truth.py. The site needs no build step and no server — every page opens directly from disk.
